# Lead brief

Goal: in one page, tell me whether this is worth an hour and what to open with.

## Gather

Work with whatever is available, and say plainly what you could not find.
Do not guess at facts — an empty line is better than an invented one.

1. The inbound message itself — read it closely. What did they actually ask for?
2. Gmail — search for prior threads with this person or their domain.
3. Google Calendar — have we met before? Is something already booked?
4. Google Drive / Notion — any existing notes, proposals, or docs on them.
5. Public info on the company: size, sector, what they sell.

## Judge

Score against the ideal-client profile in CLAUDE.md. Be honest — a clear
"probably not a fit" is more useful than a hedge.

## Output format

Exactly this shape, one page:

> **Anna Dubois — Ridgeline Logistics** (~120 employees, regional freight)
>
> **Asked for:** A "chatbot for customer service." Came in through the site form.
>
> **Fit:** Medium. Right size and they have a real volume problem, but the ask is
> a tool, not an outcome — likely hasn't scoped it internally yet.
>
> **History:** No prior email. No prior meeting.
>
> **What I'd open with:** "Before we talk about a bot — how many of those inbound
> questions are the same five questions?" Reframes it from build-me-a-thing to
> where-is-the-time-going.
>
> **Watch for:** They may be shopping three vendors on price. Ask who else they're
> talking to in the first ten minutes.
>
> **Couldn't find:** Their current tooling, whether there's a budget owner besides Anna.

## Before returning

Check: every factual claim traces to something you actually read. Anything you
inferred is labeled as inference. The "couldn't find" section is filled in
honestly, not left empty to look thorough.
